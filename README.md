This project is part of my Community Action Computing class and focuses on intentionally using what I have learned in class about 
telling a story with data to tell a misleading one. 

***
### Source of Truth
As mentioned above, this dashboard is intentionally deceptive. I began this project by 
researching the gender distribution in the workforce, finding that although much progress has been made, men still make up a bit over half of the working world. 

The [Women in the Workforce 2024 report](https://www.mckinsey.com/featured-insights/diversity-and-inclusion/women-in-the-workplace) 
by McKinsey & Company explores the ongoing challenges and slow progress toward gender equality in the corporate world, focusing on women's representation, leadership roles, and the systemic barriers that hinder their advancement, 
and was my source of truth for this project. 

### Deception 
In order to deceive my audience, I took real and credible data, then manipulated it by cutting and pasting to create misleading visuals. 
I focused on trends over a single year because I liked the pattern it created. 
I combined two graphs that didn't display the same information and was vague in my descriptions. 
I altered the scales of the graphs to make changes appear more significant.

I had a lot of fun creating these graphs and telling whatever story I wanted to convey. 
This experience has made me more critical of the data visualizations I encounter now. 
What aspects of the data are being distorted? 
After all, it's so easy—and quite enjoyable—to manipulate the narrative.

### SwD Strategies 
- **Color**: I used color to highlight certain elements (p. 120).
- **Simple Text**: I used bold percents in my grpahs to highlight the values(p. 38)
- **Simple Text (Slideshow)**: I kept text minimal in my slideshow, allowing the visuals to take center stage while leaving detailed explanations for my verbal presentation (p. 38).
 **Storyboarding**: Mapping out the flow of my story helped shape the structure of my visualization. This process led me to use a slider to display monthly trends (p. 31-33).
- **Big Idea**: I used this technique to develop a clear thesis and build my three-minute story around it (p. 31).
